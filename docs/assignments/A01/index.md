#### Portfolio 1 - Jack Stevens
https://johnduncanstevens.wixsite.com/portfolio 

Jack's portfolio is easy to navigate thanks to the six tab buttons on the top of the page. There are some concerns about the order of the tabs: the Home, Education & Experience, and Awards & Interests pages all lead to the same page and are interspersed with the other buttons. Otherwise, any user is able to locate all of Jack's engineering projects as well as his side projects in a more than reasonable time. Additionally, there is a button on the home page which takes the user to a small summary page. This page is not as exhaustive as the others but still provides great detail as well as links to specific projects.

The project pages vary in the amount of detail provided. There is a pattern in that personal projects like the 4-Cylinder Engine have less specifics compared to academic projects like the Capstone. A knowledgeable engineer would be able to comprehend and reproduce Jack's design for an aeroponics garden. He describes the project as if he is presenting it again for the website's readers. He lists his research process, the design process, testing, and ends with the final product and a prospective improvement. Jack also thoroughly detailed his chassis design for Northeastern's FSAE Racing Team. Following a similar style to the aeroponics page, Jack provides initial sketches leading to the design process. To be as helpful as possible, he takes the time to explain any concepts that may not be common knowledge. On the topic of reproducibility, Jack's projects cover a somewhat wide range of specialties which may be harder for a single person to completely grasp. For those already acquainted with motorsports design, they will be able to follow the FSAE page.

I am impressed with not only with Jack's extensive engineering work but with his website design. He maintains a light, yet professional and informative tone throughout his documentation. His home page serves as a resume which is an effective way to present your experience to peers and companies.

#### Portfolio 2 - Frederick Wachter
https://fwachter.github.io 

Frederick's website is a little older, being last updated in 2018. Due to this, some of the links to projects are now outdated or unreachable. Regardless, he has a clearly defined page showing where to find all of his previous projects and others for his internships and competitions. Possibly a symptom of its time, this website can be clunky at times. Frederick opted for a sliding page system that is controlled by page buttons at the bottom. There is a flattering design with boxes that, when hovered over, reveal a brief description and hyperlinks to the project's documentation or videos.

Frederick has extensive documentation for some of his projects but very limited explanation of others. His Swerve Robotic Platform and Soccer Robots projects have full guides while the ASME Minigolf Course only has two short YouTube videos. The projects that do include documentation are incredibly detailed. The Swerve Robotic Platform reads like a textbook chapter or an official research article. It appears to be considerably reproducible as every component is singled out and explained. Frederick includes a a multitude of graphs and images to aid his description of the project's process.

#### Product Analysis - Ratchet Wrench
This ratchet wrench is designed to increase the user's ability to quickly fasten bolts. The tool relies on its toothed drive gear to allow seamless rotational motion in a single direction at a time. The wrench has two internal pawls affixed to springs that control the movement. Each pawl is set where it clears gear teeth rotating alongside it but lock the mechanism when teeth run converse to the pawl.
